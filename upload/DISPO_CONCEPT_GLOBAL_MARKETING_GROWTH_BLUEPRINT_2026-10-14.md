# 0. EXECUTIVE DIRECTIVE

Build DISPO-CONCEPT into a credible, memorable, evidence-led global medical consumables manufacturing and supply brand originating in India. The website must feel like an international industrial-medical enterprise—not a small catalogue, generic trading page, or template medical website.

The commercial ambition is global scale. The operating principle is proof before promise.

**The strategic idea:**  
**Medical essentials. Made with purpose. Supplied with discipline.**

**The market-facing promise:**  
**A clearer, more disciplined way to source medical consumables.**

**The brand belief:**  
In healthcare procurement, confidence is not created by louder claims. It is created when a buyer can understand the product, verify the evidence, assess the supplier, and move to a qualified commercial conversation without friction.

**The desired buyer reaction:**  
“This company understands institutional procurement. It communicates clearly. It knows what it can prove, what it needs to confirm, and how to move a serious enquiry forward.”

## Non-negotiable truth policy

This blueprint does not authorise the website to claim that every product is manufactured in-house, that the company exports to every target region, that any certificate is current, or that production capacity, clinical performance, sterility, delivery times, customers, partnerships, facilities, or regulatory approvals exist unless management has approved documentary evidence.

The existing catalogue contains seven product families. Their `supplyRoleVerified` fields are currently false. Treat each product family and each SKU as a separate verification decision.

- Use **manufacturer** for a product only when the actual manufacturing entity, site, process scope and evidence are verified.
- Use **supplier** where sourcing/distribution is verified.
- Use **manufacturer and supplier** at company level only if both roles are documented and the wording clearly reflects the real operating model.
- Until verification, use the safer umbrella language: **“Medical consumables manufacturing and supply platform from India”** or **“Medical consumables for qualified B2B procurement”**, subject to management and legal review.
- Do not publish unverified ISO, CE, EO sterilisation, regulatory, product-performance or “Swiss-grade” claims as certification or independent endorsement.
- Do not use fabricated customer logos, testimonials, case studies, facility photos, production statistics, export figures, awards, scarcity, or social proof.
- Do not publish valuation, revenue, funding, margins or other financial figures without explicit written approval.
- The long-term enterprise ambition is an internal strategic direction, not a public claim about present size, valuation, capacity or market position.

# 2. PSYCHOLOGICAL CONVERSION SYSTEM

Use buyer psychology to reduce uncertainty and effort, not to manipulate clinical risk or manufacture urgency.

## 2.1 Six buyer states

1. **Attention:** “This looks like a serious international supplier.”
2. **Relevance:** “They carry categories that match my procurement needs.”
3. **Comprehension:** “I understand what they offer and what remains to be confirmed.”
4. **Confidence:** “I can inspect real evidence and understand the supplier’s operating model.”
5. **Control:** “I can ask for a quote or technical review without committing to an order.”
6. **Action:** “I know exactly what information to submit and what happens next.”

Every page and campaign should move the visitor one step forward. Do not ask for a large RFQ before showing enough context to make the request feel worthwhile.

## 2.2 Ethical psychological triggers

### A. Cognitive fluency
People trust information they can understand. Use short headlines, explicit product labels, consistent layouts, diagrams, and plain-language summaries. Never simplify away a material regulatory qualification.

**Website pattern:** “Product → Configuration → Documentation → Qualification → Enquiry.”

### B. Specificity
Specific, verifiable details are more persuasive than vague adjectives.

Weak: “World-class quality.”  
Better: “Request the current product specification, applicable conformity documents and available lot/traceability information for the item you are evaluating.”  
Publish the documents only when they exist, are current, and are approved for release.

### C. Risk reduction
Procurement buyers need to reduce supplier, quality, compliance, continuity and communication risk.

**Proof modules:** manufacturing/supply role, product specifications, quality process, document control, complaint handling, traceability, regulatory scope, sample/qualification path and escalation contact—each displayed only when verified.

### D. Competence signals
Use an orderly technical design system, exact terminology, accessible documents, disciplined forms and precise response expectations. Avoid decorative seals, fake badges and borrowed national prestige.

### E. Autonomy and control
Let buyers choose the enquiry path: product quote, OEM/private label, distributor discussion, technical documentation, tender/institutional procurement or general supplier qualification.

### F. Reciprocity
Provide a useful RFQ preparation checklist, product comparison worksheet or supplier qualification pack before asking for extensive information. Do not gate essential safety or regulatory information behind marketing capture.

### G. Commitment through useful next steps
Ask for a small first action: select a product family, state the target market, or request a document review. Make the next step explicit and reversible.

### H. Authentic social proof
Add customer references, audit outcomes, approved case studies, repeat-order evidence and verified partnerships only after permission and evidence are obtained. Until then, use process evidence and product documentation—not invented testimonials.

### I. Responsible urgency
Use genuine, dated deadlines only when a real tender, trade event, regulatory transition or buyer-supplied schedule warrants them. No fake countdown timers, artificial scarcity or unsupported “limited stock” messages.

## 2.3 Buyer friction to remove

- Do not make visitors guess whether a product is manufactured or sourced.
- Do not require registration just to view a product category.
- Do not force all visitors into one generic contact form.
- Do not request unnecessary personal information.
- Do not promise instant quotes when technical review is required.
- Do not claim a response SLA until the team can meet it.
- Do not use an AI chatbot as a substitute for a qualified human on regulatory or technical decisions.
- Explain why each required RFQ field is needed.
- Preserve form progress where safe and appropriate; clearly state file types and limits.
- Offer a low-friction “I’m still evaluating” path.

# 9. RFQ CONVERSION DESIGN

The current application already has an RFQ endpoint, Zod validation, a same-origin check, honeypot/minimum-time controls, an in-memory rate limit, private file storage and Prisma records. Preserve these protections while hardening production.

## 9.1 Form design

Step 1 — What are you enquiring about?
- Product family.
- Product variant if known.
- Enquiry type: quotation / documentation / OEM-private label / distribution / institutional tender / other.

Step 2 — Where will it be used?
- Destination country/market.
- Buyer organisation type.
- Intended application or procurement context.
- Relevant standards/documents, if known.

Step 3 — What do you need?
- Approximate demand range, optional if unknown.
- Required configuration.
- Packaging/label needs, if applicable.
- Target timing supplied by the buyer, not a promised company lead time.

Step 4 — How can we respond?
- Name.
- Work email.
- Company.
- Country.
- Phone, optional unless needed.
- Preferred language.
- Message.
